两周课堂Two Weeks in the Classroom

本课目标：掌握海龟课程的第一性框架——五个问题、比较原则和无记忆交易，理解为什么这门课从“怎么亏钱”讲起。

一、第一天的意外：先讲风险#

1984 年 1 月，芝加哥联合联盟俱乐部。第一天的教室安静得能听见针掉在地上。所有人都在等丹尼斯讲“怎么赚大钱”。

丹尼斯没来。走上讲台的是埃克哈特，开口的第一主题是：管理风险。

对一个刚入门的交易者，这完全不合常理。大家想学的是买什么、什么时候买；老师讲的却是“亏钱的时候你该怎么办”。但这正是整门课的地基：先保证自己能留在牌桌上，再谈赢钱。

二、五个问题：每天开盘前必须答完#

埃克哈特把交易压缩成五个问题，语气里没有任何商量余地：

  1. 市场现在是什么状态？ —— 价格是多少。不是“应该值多少”，是此刻成交在什么位置。
  2. 市场的波动率是多少？ —— 这个市场一天通常上下走多少。海龟给这个数字起了个名字：N。
  3. 你现在有多少权益（本金）？ —— 不是去年有多少，不是峰值有多少，是现在账户里有多少钱。
  4. 你用哪套系统？ —— 开盘前就必须有明确的买卖计划，不能临场拍脑袋：“它有 500 了，我该买还是该卖？”
  5. 你能承受多大风险？ —— 每笔交易愿意冒账户的百分之几。

前两个问题是客观事实：价格就是价格，N 就是 N，谁看都一样。后三个问题是当下决策：我有多少钱、我按什么规则做、我冒多大险。

注意第 3 和第 5 个问题的共同点：它们都只跟“现在”有关。五年前你有一百万，今天只剩五十万，你不能拿“我曾经有一百万”来做决策。你只能拿现在的五十万做决策。

三、比较原则：你不特殊#

埃克哈特反复讲一个思想实验：

两个交易者，权益相同、系统相同、风险偏好相同，面对完全相同的市场情形。那么对其中一个人最优的做法，对另一个人也一定是最优的。

听上去是废话，但人性恰好相反。大多数人遇到相同情形，总想“我能不能想得比别人多一点、做得比别人巧一点”。而在机械交易里，这种“多一点”就是偏离规则，就是风险。

埃克哈特的意思可以翻译成一句很直白的话：

你不特殊。你不比市场聪明。所以照规则做。你是谁、多聪明，一点都不重要。

丹尼斯说得更狠：“遵循好原则，不让恐惧、贪婪和希望干扰交易，是很难的。你是在逆着人性游泳。”

四、无记忆交易#

埃克哈特要求海龟用“无记忆”的方式交易：

你不该关心自己是怎么走到当前状态的，而该关心现在应该做什么。一个因为信心起伏而改变做法的交易者，关注的是自己的过去，而不是当下的现实。

这条规则直接切断了几种最常见的自我毁灭方式：

  • 上一笔亏了，所以这一笔不敢做——用过去的伤痛否决现在的信号。
  • 上一笔赚了，所以这一笔加倍做——把运气当成能力。
  • 连着赚了三个月，觉得自己“手感来了”——把系统优势误认成个人天赋。
  • 账户从一百万跌到五十万，仍然按一百万的规模下注——活在已经不存在的账户里。

无记忆不等于不学习。它指的是：评估信号时，不要带入情绪账本。 系统该怎么算就怎么算，你要做的是执行，而不是给自己讲故事。

五、课堂的真实样子#

多年传说把这两周描述得神乎其神，实际上相当朴素：

  • 地点是联合联盟俱乐部，老派绅士俱乐部，所有人必须穿西装打领带，丹尼斯也不例外。
  • 第一天由戴尔·德卢特里（Dale Dellutri）讲流程和“家务事”，他是海龟们的“后勤妈妈”。
  • 主讲是埃克哈特，不是丹尼斯。“肉和土豆”大部分是埃克哈特教的，丹尼斯补充的是一连串交易战场上的故事。
  • 另一位同事罗伯特·莫斯（Robert Moss）专门讲订单执行：你的单子进了交易池之后到底发生了什么。
  • 形式主要是讲授加记笔记，允许提问，但一开始几乎没人敢问。
  • 第二年那期培训缩短为一周。

这两周没有励志演讲，没有“相信自己”，没有“跟着感觉走”。有的是规则、算术、例子，以及一遍又一遍的“照做”。

六、两个人的分工#

迈克·香农的评价很到位：很多系统的开发其实不是丹尼斯，而是埃克哈特；两人一起把它“孵”了出来。

  • 丹尼斯：直觉极强，能在瞬间看出一件事意味着什么。埃克哈特都惊叹他那种“看一眼就懂”的能力。
  • 埃克哈特：数学和概率上的真正高手，负责把直觉变成可检验、可执行的规则。

一个负责“看见”，一个负责“证明”。这个组合，才是海龟实验真正的引擎。

本课要点#

  • 课程从风险管理开始，而不是从赚钱开始：先活下来。
  • 五个问题：市场状态、波动率（N）、当前权益、系统、风险承受度。
  • 前两个是客观事实，后三个是当下决策，历史答案没有意义。
  • 比较原则：条件相同的交易者，最优决策必须相同——你不特殊。
  • 无记忆交易：不看自己怎么走到今天，只看现在该做什么。
  • 课堂内容朴素：规则、算术、执行细节，没有励志和玄学。

自测#

  1. 为什么“我有多少钱”和“我愿意冒多大险”都必须用当下的数字，而不能用历史峰值？
  2. 一个交易者连续三个月盈利后开始加大仓位，说“我现在状态很好”。用无记忆原则分析这句话的问题。
  3. 埃克哈特的“比较原则”如果被严格执行，会怎样消灭“这次不一样”的借口？
参考答案

1. 仓位规则按当前权益计算。用历史峰值会让实际风险超过计划风险：账户缩水后仍按旧规模下注，同样的行情会造成更大比例的亏损。

2. 他把近期结果当成了能力证据，让过去的盈利进入了当下的决策。系统信号并没有变化，变化的只是他的情绪；按无记忆原则，仓位应由权益和 N 决定，而不是由“手感”决定。

3. 比较原则要求：给定相同的权益、系统、风险偏好和市场情形，决策必须相同。任何“这次不一样”的说法，都必须指出这四个变量中究竟哪一个真的变了；如果都变了，规则自然会给出不同答案，不需要额外的主观判断。

What you will learn: the first-principles framework of the Turtle course — the five questions, the comparison principle and memory-less trading — and why the class began with losing money.

1. A surprise on day one: risk first#

January 1984, the Union League Club in Chicago. On the first day you could hear a pin drop. Everyone was waiting for Dennis to explain how to get rich.

Dennis was not there. Eckhardt took the floor, and his first topic was managing risk.

For a beginner this makes no sense. People come to learn what to buy and when; the teacher talks about what you do when you lose. But this is the foundation of the entire method: stay in the game first, talk about winning later.

2. Five questions to answer before the open#

Eckhardt compressed trading into five questions, with no room for negotiation:

  1. What is the state of the market? — The price. Not what it "should" be worth, but where it is trading right now.
  2. What is the volatility of the market? — How much it typically moves in a day. The Turtles gave this number a name: N.
  3. What is the equity being traded? — Not what you had last year or at the peak, but what is in the account now.
  4. What is the system or trading orientation? — You must have a battle plan before the open. You cannot wake up and improvise: "It's at 500 — do I buy or sell?"
  5. What is the risk aversion of the trader? — What percentage of the account you are willing to risk per trade.

The first two are objective facts: the price is the price, N is N, everyone sees the same thing. The last three are present-tense decisions: how much I have, what rules I follow, how much I risk.

Note what questions 3 and 5 share: they concern only now. Five years ago you had a million; today you have half a million. You cannot make decisions based on the million you used to have. You can only decide with the half million you have now.

3. The comparison principle: you are not special#

Eckhardt returned again and again to a thought experiment:

Two traders with the same equity, the same system and the same risk aversion, facing exactly the same market situation. Whatever is optimal for one must be optimal for the other.

It sounds trivial, but human nature runs the other way. Most people, facing the same situation, want to think a little harder, add a little twist. In mechanical trading, that "little twist" is a deviation from the rules — and deviation is risk.

Eckhardt's point translates bluntly:

You are not special. You are not smarter than the market. So follow the rules. Who you are and how smart you are makes no difference.

Dennis put it more starkly: "To follow the good principles and not let fear, greed and hope interfere with your trading is tough. You're swimming upstream against human nature."

4. Memory-less trading#

Eckhardt demanded that the Turtles trade without memory:

You shouldn't care about how you got to the current state but rather about what you should do now. A trader who trades differentially because of swings in confidence is focusing on his or her own past rather than on current realities.

This rule cuts off the classic forms of self-destruction:

  • Last trade lost, so I skip this signal — letting an old wound veto a present signal.
  • Last trade won, so I double up — mistaking luck for skill.
  • Three good months, so "I've got the touch" — mistaking system edge for personal genius.
  • The account fell from a million to half a million, but I still size as if I had a million — trading an account that no longer exists.

Memory-less does not mean no learning. It means: when you evaluate a signal, leave the emotional ledger out of it. The system calculates; your job is to execute, not to narrate.

5. What the classroom was actually like#

Legend has made those two weeks sound far more glamorous than they were:

  • The venue was the Union League Club, an old-fashioned gentlemen's club. Jackets and ties at all times — including Dennis.
  • Day one was Dale Dellutri on procedures and "housekeeping"; he was the Turtles' team mom.
  • The main teacher was Eckhardt, not Dennis. Much of the meat and potatoes was Eckhardt's; Dennis supplied a stream of war stories.
  • Another colleague, Robert Moss, taught order execution — what actually happens in the pit once your order arrives.
  • The format was lecture and note-taking, with questions allowed but few asked at first.
  • The following year's class was cut to one week.

No motivational speeches, no "believe in yourself," no "follow your gut." Rules, arithmetic, examples, and "do it" over and over.

6. Who taught what#

Michael Shannon's assessment is apt: much of the system development was not Dennis but Eckhardt; the two hatched it together.

  • Dennis had extraordinary intuition, seeing in an instant what something meant. Even Eckhardt marvelled at his ability to look at an idea and say, "Look what this means. It works."
  • Eckhardt was the mathematician, the master of probability, who turned intuition into testable, executable rules.

One saw; the other proved. That combination was the real engine of the Turtle experiment.

Key points#

  • The course began with risk, not profit: survive first.
  • The five questions: market state, volatility (N), current equity, system, risk tolerance.
  • The first two are objective facts; the last three are present decisions. History is irrelevant.
  • Comparison principle: identical conditions demand identical decisions — you are not special.
  • Memory-less trading: ignore how you got here; act on what is true now.
  • The classroom was plain: rules, arithmetic, execution, no mystique.

Self-test#

  1. Why must "how much I have" and "how much I risk" use present numbers rather than historical peaks?
  2. A trader has three profitable months and increases size, saying "I'm in great form." Analyse that with the memory-less principle.
  3. If the comparison principle were enforced strictly, how would it eliminate the excuse "this time is different"?
Answers

1. Position rules scale off current equity. Sizing off a past peak makes actual risk exceed planned risk: after a drawdown, the same market move produces a larger percentage loss.

2. He is treating recent results as evidence of ability, letting past profits enter a present decision. The signal is unchanged; only his mood has changed. Under memory-less trading, size follows equity and N, not "form."

3. It requires that identical equity, system, risk aversion and market situation produce identical decisions. Any claim that "this time is different" must name which of those four variables actually changed. If one did, the rules already produce a different answer without subjective judgment.
