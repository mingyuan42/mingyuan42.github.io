THE COMPLETE TURTLETRADER · A BILINGUAL COURSE

A real experiment,
turned into a learnable craft

In 1983, Chicago's trading king Richard Dennis ran a classified ad and taught a group of novices to trade trends in two weeks. This course breaks the story, the philosophy and the full rule set into 14 lessons and 2 appendices, in Chinese and English.

In the autumn of 1983, Chicago trader Richard Dennis placed a small ad in the Wall Street Journal: commodity futures traders wanted, no experience necessary, proprietary methods taught. He and his partner William Eckhardt had argued for a decade about whether trading could be taught. Dennis said yes. Eckhardt said no. So they settled it with people.

The people selected became known as the Turtles. Over the next five years, a group of former accountants, students, musicians and chess players traded Dennis's money with Dennis's rules and made tens of millions of dollars. After the program ended, some became billion-dollar fund managers; others quietly disappeared; a few sold the rules as books and courses.

This course is not a retelling of the legend. It takes the legend apart: what exactly were they taught, why did those rules work, and how can someone who knows nothing today actually learn them?

How to use this course#

The course runs in four parts — story, philosophy, rules, aftermath — with 14 lessons and 2 appendices. Every lesson has a goal, a full explanation, a key-points recap and a self-test. Worked answers to all calculation questions are in Appendix B.

  • First pass: read in order. Lessons 6–8 are the foundation for the rules; Lessons 9–13 are where the numbers begin.
  • Second pass: read only the "Key points" at the end of each lesson, then return to the paragraphs you could not explain in your own words.
  • Do the arithmetic: with paper, follow Lessons 9–12 and calculate N, contract size, stop price and pyramiding profit yourself. Understanding and calculating are different skills.
  • Do not rush to trade: this is a course about how a method works, not a promise of returns. In real markets, following rules is far harder than understanding them.

Three warnings#

First, the rules are not a holy grail. The heart of Turtle trading is not the number 20 or 55. It is: follow trends, diversify, size positions by volatility, shrink when losing, add when winning. Parameters can change; the logic cannot.

Second, the success stories contain survivorship bias. We remember Parker and Abraham; we rarely discuss the people who left. Lesson 14 addresses this directly.

Third, this is study material. It is not investment advice and promises no return. Futures and leveraged trading can lose more than the capital committed. If you ever practise, begin with very small size and a long paper record, and first find out whether you can follow rules at all.
