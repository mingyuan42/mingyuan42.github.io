破产风险——你会活着，还是死掉？Risk of Ruin — Will You Live or Die?

本课目标：理解为什么“正期望”还不够，学会用减仓规则把“破产概率”压到接近零，并算清回撤与恢复的不对称。

一、期望值回答不了的问题#

上一课我们算出：一套正期望的系统，长期一定赚钱。

但“长期一定赚钱”有一个前提：你得活到长期。

想象两个人用同一套系统：

  • A 每笔冒账户的 2%。
  • B 每笔冒账户的 60%。

系统完全一样，期望值完全一样。但 B 只要连续遇到几次正常范围内的亏损，账户就归零了——归零之后，再好的期望值也与他无关。

所以风险管理要回答的不是“能不能赚”，而是：“我会不会在中途死掉？”

二、亏损是线性的，盈利是指数的#

埃克哈特有个很关键的观察：不要让海龟们害怕账户的线性下降，因为一次大趋势带来的指数上升，最终会超过他们亏损时看到的最陡的线性下跌。

这句话需要拆开看：

  • 亏损的日常形态是线性的：一笔亏 2%，再一笔亏 2%，账户一点点往下磨。看起来漫长、难受、没有尽头。
  • 盈利的形态是指数的：大部分时间不赚钱，一旦趋势展开，几周内的复利增长可以非常陡。

这就像挖隧道：前面九十米都是黑的，最后一米见光。如果你在第八十九米退出，前面所有的坚持都归零。

趋势跟踪者的日常，就是长时间承受线性磨损，等待那一次指数跳跃。

三、下注太大，会毁掉正期望#

用一个极简模型感受一下“下注比例”的威力。

假设你每次把全部资金押上，哪怕胜率 60%、赔率 1:1：

  • 赢一次，资金翻倍。
  • 输一次，资金归零。

只要交易次数足够多，归零几乎必然发生。期望值为正，破产概率却是 100%。

再假设每次押 50%：

  • 连输两次：剩 25%。
  • 连输三次：剩 12.5%。

要回到原点，你得连赢三次（12.5% → 25% → 50% → 100%）。而连输三次的概率，远比直觉高。

这就是为什么海龟只用 2%：不是因为 2% 最优，而是因为它让“连续亏损十几次仍然活着”成为常态。

一个粗略的算术：每笔亏 2%，连亏 10 笔，账户还剩 0.98¹⁰ ≈ 81.7%；连亏 20 笔，还剩约 66.8%。虽然难受，但远未出局。而每笔亏 20%，连亏 10 笔只剩 10.7%。

四、海龟的减仓规则#

海龟不只是“每笔 2%”这么简单。他们还有一条随回撤自动减速的规则：

账户每回撤 10%，就把每单位的风险降低 20%。

以 2% 为起点：

从峰值回撤 单位风险 计算
0% 2.00% 起点
10% 1.60% 2.00 × 0.8
20% 1.28% 1.60 × 0.8
30% 1.024% 1.28 × 0.8
40% 0.819% 1.024 × 0.8

注意这条规则的两个特征：

  1. 它是预先写死的，不靠临场判断。你不需要在亏损时“冷静地做个决定”，规则已经替你决定了。
  2. 它同时降低风险和恢复难度：亏损时暴露变小，账户不会加速失血；等权益回升，仓位逐步恢复，趋势来临时你仍然在场。

当资金重新上涨、回撤收窄，单位规模就逐步调回正常。海龟不是“亏了就永久缩手”，而是跟着权益走。

五、代价：难受，而且看起来“很慢”#

减仓不是免费的。它带来两个心理成本：

  • 回本变慢：仓位小了，同样的行情赚得少。
  • 自我怀疑：连续几个月缩着做，会觉得自己“错过了”。

厄尔·基弗记得有位同行哀叹：“天啊，我亏太多了，我得赚 100% 才能回本。”但那个人最后年底拿到了不错的奖金——因为市场终于开始联动、开始走趋势。基弗说：当统计终于起作用、所有市场开始动起来时，那些“热线”能把你从回撤里飞快地拉出来。

海龟的真实记录更能说明问题：他们可能连续十一个月亏损，然后在最后三周里，从 −30% 或 −40% 一路做到 +150%。前提是，那三周到来时，他们还在场上，而且手里还有仓位。

六、把规则变成习惯#

这一课可以浓缩成三个动作：

  1. 先定最大可承受回撤，再反推每笔风险。如果你只能接受 20% 的回撤，就不要用会带来 50% 回撤的仓位。
  2. 把减仓规则写在纸上，包括触发条件和具体数字。情绪上头时，人不会做算术，只会做决定。
  3. 区分“系统失效”和“系统在亏损”。前者需要证据（期望值是否仍为正、规则是否被正确执行），后者是系统的正常呼吸。

本课要点#

  • 正期望不足以保证赚钱，必须先保证活到期望兑现。
  • 亏损通常线性累积，趋势盈利可能指数跳升；关键是不要在跳跃前出局。
  • 下注比例过大时，正期望系统的破产概率仍可接近 100%。
  • 海龟规则：每回撤 10%，单位风险降 20%（2% → 1.6% → 1.28% → …）。
  • 减仓会自动降低失血速度，并在权益恢复时逐步加回暴露。
  • 代价是恢复慢、心理难受；收益是极大概率活到趋势到来。

自测#

  1. 为什么“每笔 2%”比“每笔 20%”更可能让一个正期望系统最终赚到钱？
  2. 一个账户从 100 万回撤到 50 万，需要上涨多少才回到原点？如果回撤到 20 万呢？
  3. 减仓规则最大的心理难点是什么？为什么“预先写死”能缓解它？
参考答案

1. 因为破产是吸收态：一旦归零，后续期望值再高也无法参与。2% 让连续亏损成为可承受的波动，20% 让几次正常亏损就接近出局，从而大幅提高“在优势兑现前死亡”的概率。

2. 从 50 万回到 100 万需要 +100%；从 20 万回到 100 万需要 +400%。亏损越深，恢复所需收益越呈非线性放大。

3. 难点是在最难受、最想“扳回来”的时候主动缩小仓位，这与本能相反。预先写死规则把决策从情绪现场移到冷静时刻，减少临场自我谈判的空间。

What you will learn: why positive expectation is not enough, how a de-risking rule pushes ruin probability toward zero, and how asymmetric drawdown recovery really is.

1. The question expectation cannot answer#

Last lesson we showed that a positive-expectation system makes money over the long run.

But "over the long run" has a precondition: you have to be alive when the long run arrives.

Imagine two people using the same system:

  • A risks 2% of the account per trade.
  • B risks 60% per trade.

Same system, same expectation. But a few ordinary losses in a row take B to zero — and at zero, expectation is irrelevant.

So risk management does not answer "can this make money?" It answers: "Will I die on the way?"

2. Losses are linear; profits can be exponential#

Eckhardt made a crucial observation: he did not want the Turtles to fear the linear decline of their accounts, because the exponential rise from one big trend would eventually outrun the steepest linear decline they experienced while losing.

Unpack it:

  • Day-to-day losses are linear: 2% here, 2% there, a slow grind downward. It feels endless.
  • Profits are exponential: nothing for a long time, then a trend unfolds and compounding turns vertical within weeks.

It is like digging a tunnel: ninety metres of darkness, then light at the last metre. Quit at metre eighty-nine and everything you endured counts for nothing.

A trend follower's daily life is enduring linear erosion while waiting for the exponential jump.

3. Betting too big destroys a positive edge#

A minimal model shows the power of bet sizing.

Suppose you stake everything each time, even with a 60% win rate at even odds:

  • Win once: double.
  • Lose once: zero.

Given enough trades, ruin is nearly certain. Positive expectation, 100% probability of ruin.

Suppose you stake 50%:

  • Two losses in a row: 25% left.
  • Three losses: 12.5% left.

To get back you need three wins in a row (12.5% → 25% → 50% → 100%). Three losses in a row is far more common than intuition suggests.

This is why the Turtles used 2%: not because 2% is optimal, but because it makes "fifteen losses in a row and still alive" a normal event.

Rough arithmetic: losing 2% ten times leaves 0.98¹⁰ ≈ 81.7% of the account; twenty times leaves about 66.8%. Painful, but far from dead. Losing 20% ten times leaves 10.7%.

4. The Turtles' de-risking rule#

The Turtles did more than risk 2% per trade. They had a rule that automatically slowed them down as drawdowns grew:

For every 10% drawdown in the account, cut unit risk by 20%.

Starting from 2%:

Drawdown from peak Unit risk Calculation
0% 2.00% starting point
10% 1.60% 2.00 × 0.8
20% 1.28% 1.60 × 0.8
30% 1.024% 1.28 × 0.8
40% 0.819% 1.024 × 0.8

Two features matter:

  1. It is written down in advance. You do not need to make a calm decision while losing; the rule has already decided.
  2. It reduces both the bleeding and the difficulty of recovery. Exposure shrinks during losses, then rebuilds as equity recovers, so you are still there when the trend arrives.

As capital rises and the drawdown closes, unit size is restored. The Turtles did not shrink forever; they followed equity.

5. The cost: it hurts, and it looks slow#

De-risking is not free. Two psychological costs:

  • Slower recovery: smaller size means smaller gains in the same move.
  • Self-doubt: months of trading small makes you feel you are missing out.

Erle Keefer remembered a peer lamenting, "Oh my God, I am down so much that I have to make 100 percent just to get back to even." That Turtle still ended the year with a good bonus, because the markets finally started clicking and trending. Keefer's point: when the statistics finally work and every market starts moving, those "hot wires" can pull you out of a drawdown fast.

The real record is starker: the Turtles could be down for eleven months, then go from −30% or −40% to +150% in the final three weeks. Provided they were still there, still holding positions, when those weeks arrived.

6. Turn the rule into a habit#

Three actions:

  1. Set the maximum acceptable drawdown first, then back into per-trade risk. If you can only tolerate 20%, do not size for 50%.
  2. Write the de-risking rule down, with triggers and exact numbers. In emotional moments people do not calculate; they decide.
  3. Separate "the system is broken" from "the system is losing." The first needs evidence (is the edge still positive, were the rules executed?); the second is normal breathing.

Key points#

  • Positive expectation is not enough; survival comes first.
  • Losses compound linearly, trend profits can jump exponentially; do not exit before the jump.
  • Oversized bets can make ruin nearly certain even with a positive edge.
  • Turtle rule: every 10% drawdown cuts unit risk by 20% (2% → 1.6% → 1.28% → …).
  • De-risking slows the bleed and rebuilds exposure as equity recovers.
  • The cost is slow recovery and discomfort; the benefit is being alive when the trend comes.

Self-test#

  1. Why is risking 2% per trade more likely to let a positive-expectation system actually pay off than risking 20%?
  2. An account falls from 1,000,000 to 500,000. What gain returns it to even? What if it falls to 200,000?
  3. What is the hardest psychological part of de-risking, and why does writing the rule down in advance help?
Answers

1. Ruin is an absorbing state: at zero you can no longer participate, whatever the edge. 2% turns a losing streak into tolerable volatility; 20% turns a few ordinary losses into near-ruin, sharply raising the chance of dying before the edge pays.

2. 500,000 → 1,000,000 requires +100%; 200,000 → 1,000,000 requires +400%. The deeper the drawdown, the nonlinearly larger the required return.

3. It means shrinking exactly when you most want to win it back, which is anti-instinctive. A pre-written rule moves the decision from the emotional moment to a calm one and removes room for self-negotiation.
