练习与答案Exercises and Answers

18 道题，覆盖波动率、仓位、止损、加仓、期望值、组合与判断。建议先自己做，再对照答案。

第一部分：N 与仓位#

第 1 题#

某市场昨天收盘 50，今天最高 56、最低 47。今天的真实波幅是多少？

答案

TR1 = 56 − 47 = 9；TR2 = |50 − 56| = 6；TR3 = |50 − 47| = 3。TR = max(9, 6, 3) = 9。

第 2 题#

过去 20 个交易日的 TR 之和为 240。N 是多少？

答案

N = 240 ÷ 20 = 12。

第 3 题#

玉米 N = 12 美分，一手 5,000 蒲式耳，1 美分 = 50 美元。1N 和 2N 分别是多少美元？

答案

1N = 12 × 50 = 600 美元；2N = 600 × 2 = 1,200 美元。

第 4 题#

账户 120,000 美元，单笔风险 2%，2N 美元风险为 600 美元。应做几手？

答案

可承受风险 = 120,000 × 2% = 2,400；2,400 ÷ 600 = 4，正好 4 手。

第 5 题#

账户 60,000 美元，单笔风险 2%，某市场 1N = 800 美元，用 2N 止损。算出的手数是多少？应该怎么做？

答案

可承受风险 = 1,200；2N = 1,600；1,200 ÷ 1,600 = 0.75，向下取整为 0 手。说明该市场波动过大，2% 预算内连一手都承担不起，正确做法是放弃交易。

第二部分：止损与加仓#

第 6 题#

做多入场 50，N = 1.5。2N 硬止损在哪里？

答案

2N = 3，止损 = 50 − 3 = 47。

第 7 题#

同上，N = 1.5，入场 50 做多。最多可以加到哪几个价位？

答案

每 1N 加一次，1N = 1.5：51.5、53、54.5、56。加上首仓共 5 个单位（4 次加仓），到 56 后不再加。

第 8 题#

在 53 加了第三个单位（N 仍为 1.5）。此时所有单位的止损应该设在哪里？

答案

按最新单位的 2N 止损：53 − 3 = 50。单位一（50 入场）、单位二（51.5 入场）、单位三（53 入场）的止损全部上移到 50。

第 9 题#

N = 1，五个单位分别在 100、101、102、103、104 入场，统一止损在 102，每点 500 美元。若全部止损，净结果是多少？

答案

逐笔：100→102 = +2 点；101→102 = +1 点；102→102 = 0；103→102 = −1 点；104→102 = −2 点。合计 0 点 = 0 美元。这说明持续上移止损后，五手满仓的最坏情况可能只是打平。

第 10 题#

单位风险为 2%，账户从峰值回撤 30%。按海龟规则，此时单位风险是多少？

答案

每回撤 10% 降 20%，30% 回撤跨过两个完整档位（10%、20%）：2% × 0.8 × 0.8 = 1.28%。要到 40% 回撤才会再降到 1.024%。

第三部分：期望值与破产风险#

第 11 题#

一套系统胜率 40%，平均盈利 1,000，平均亏损 300。期望值是多少？

答案

E = 0.40 × 1,000 − 0.60 × 300 = 400 − 180 = 220 美元/笔。

第 12 题#

平均盈利 600，平均亏损 450。盈亏平衡胜率是多少？

答案

450 ÷ (600 + 450) = 450 ÷ 1,050 ≈ 42.9%。胜率高于 42.9% 才长期赚钱。

第 13 题#

某方法胜率 90%，每次赚 40 美元，亏的时候亏 600 美元。它的期望值是多少？结论是什么？

答案

E = 0.90 × 40 − 0.10 × 600 = 36 − 60 = −24 美元/笔。高胜率掩盖了负期望：长期必亏，而且会在一次大亏中回吐大量小赢。

第四部分：组合与判断#

第 14 题#

组合有 7 个多头单位、4 个空头单位。按海龟公式，净风险是多少？

答案

7 − (4 ÷ 2) = 5 个单位。

第 15 题#

组合有 3 个多头单位、8 个空头单位。净风险是多少？

答案

8 − (3 ÷ 2) = 6.5 个单位。

第 16 题#

组合持有：道指多 1、标普多 1、黄金多 1、白银多 1、日元空 1。用净单位公式算总风险，并指出这个数字忽略了什么。

答案

公式：4 − (1 ÷ 2) = 3.5 个单位。但它忽略了相关性：道指与标普是同一个风险源，黄金与白银是同一个风险源。所以真正的独立风险源只有三个（股指、贵金属、日元空头），实际分散度远低于“五个市场”的表象。净单位公式和相关性判断必须一起用。

第 17 题#

上一次 S1 的 20 日突破信号，你没有真的下单，但它理论上会盈利。现在又出现一个 20 日突破信号，你该怎么做？如果行情继续走，你还有机会吗？

答案

应当跳过——过滤器不看你是否真的交易，只看上一次信号是否“理论上盈利”。如果行情继续，S2 的 55 日突破会提供第二次入场机会，这也是“失败安全阀”的意义。

第 18 题#

系统 A 在历史回测中极其漂亮，但把 17/9 改成 18/10 后绩效崩溃；系统 B 用 20/10 与 55/20，参数小幅调整后表现接近。你更该相信哪一个？为什么？

答案

相信 B。参数微调就导致绩效崩溃，说明 A 的收益依赖极窄的参数区间，极可能是对历史数据的过度拟合，实盘环境稍变就会失效。B 表现出稳健性（robustness）：参数小幅变动下结果相近，更可能捕捉到真实的市场结构，而不是噪音。

评分参考#

  • 14–18 题正确：你已经掌握了海龟系统的核心算术，可以开始纸上练习。
  • 10–13 题正确：回看第 9–13 课，重点补 N、仓位与加仓。
  • 10 题以下：不要急着实盘。先把附录 A 的规则抄一遍，再用历史数据手工推演十笔交易。

Eighteen problems covering volatility, sizing, stops, pyramiding, expectation, portfolio construction and judgement. Try them first, then check the answers.

Part 1: N and position sizing#

Problem 1#

A market closed at 50 yesterday; today's high is 56 and low is 47. What is today's true range?

Answer

TR1 = 56 − 47 = 9; TR2 = |50 − 56| = 6; TR3 = |50 − 47| = 3. TR = max(9, 6, 3) = 9.

Problem 2#

The last 20 daily TRs sum to 240. What is N?

Answer

N = 240 ÷ 20 = 12.

Problem 3#

Corn has N = 12 cents; one contract is 5,000 bushels and 1 cent = $50. What are 1N and 2N in dollars?

Answer

1N = 12 × 50 = $600; 2N = 600 × 2 = $1,200.

Problem 4#

Equity is $120,000, risk per trade 2%, and the 2N dollar risk is $600. How many contracts?

Answer

Allowed risk = 120,000 × 2% = 2,400; 2,400 ÷ 600 = 4, so 4 contracts.

Problem 5#

Equity $60,000, risk 2%, a market with 1N = $800, using a 2N stop. What is the contract count, and what should you do?

Answer

Allowed risk = 1,200; 2N = 1,600; 1,200 ÷ 1,600 = 0.75, rounded down to 0 contracts. The market is too volatile to fit even one contract inside the 2% budget; the correct action is to skip the trade.

Part 2: Stops and pyramiding#

Problem 6#

Long entry at 50 with N = 1.5. Where is the 2N hard stop?

Answer

2N = 3, so the stop is 50 − 3 = 47.

Problem 7#

Same trade: N = 1.5, long entry at 50. At which prices can you add, and how many units in total?

Answer

Every 1N = 1.5: adds at 51.5, 53, 54.5 and 56. With the first unit that is 5 units (four adds); no more after 56.

Problem 8#

A third unit was added at 53 (N still 1.5). Where should every stop now sit?

Answer

At the newest unit's 2N stop: 53 − 3 = 50. Units entered at 50, 51.5 and 53 all move their stops to 50.

Problem 9#

N = 1; five units enter at 100, 101, 102, 103 and 104; all stops sit at 102; each point is worth $500. If everything is stopped out, what is the net result?

Answer

Unit by unit: 100→102 = +2 pts; 101→102 = +1; 102→102 = 0; 103→102 = −1; 104→102 = −2. Net 0 points = $0. With stops ratcheting upward, a fully loaded five-unit position can have a worst case of merely breaking even.

Problem 10#

Unit risk is 2% and the account is 30% below its peak. Under the Turtle rule, what is unit risk now?

Answer

Cut 20% per 10% drawdown; 30% crosses two full tiers (10% and 20%): 2% × 0.8 × 0.8 = 1.28%. It only falls to 1.024% at a 40% drawdown.

Part 3: Expectation and risk of ruin#

Problem 11#

A system wins 40% of the time, average winner 1,000, average loser 300. What is its expectation?

Answer

E = 0.40 × 1,000 − 0.60 × 300 = 400 − 180 = $220 per trade.

Problem 12#

Average winner 600, average loser 450. What is the break-even win rate?

Answer

450 ÷ (600 + 450) = 450 ÷ 1,050 ≈ 42.9%. Above that, the system is profitable over time.

Problem 13#

A method wins 90% of the time, making $40 on a win and losing $600 on a loss. What is its expectation, and what do you conclude?

Answer

E = 0.90 × 40 − 0.10 × 600 = 36 − 60 = −$24 per trade. High accuracy masks negative expectation: it loses over time, and one big loss gives back many small wins.

Part 4: Portfolio and judgement#

Problem 14#

A portfolio holds 7 long units and 4 short units. What is net risk under the Turtle formula?

Answer

7 − (4 ÷ 2) = 5 units.

Problem 15#

A portfolio holds 3 long units and 8 short units. What is net risk?

Answer

8 − (3 ÷ 2) = 6.5 units.

Problem 16#

A portfolio holds: Dow long 1, S&P long 1, gold long 1, silver long 1, yen short 1. Compute net units and explain what that number ignores.

Answer

Formula: 4 − (1 ÷ 2) = 3.5 units. But it ignores correlation: the Dow and S&P are one risk source, gold and silver are another. So the true independent sources are only three (equity, metals, short yen), and diversification is far weaker than "five markets" suggests. Net units and correlation judgement must be used together.

Problem 17#

The last S1 20-day breakout was not traded but would theoretically have won. A new 20-day breakout appears. What do you do? If the move continues, do you still get a chance?

Answer

Skip it — the filter counts theoretical winners regardless of whether you traded. If the move continues, S2's 55-day breakout provides a second entry; that is the purpose of the fail-safe.

Problem 18#

System A looks spectacular in backtest but collapses when 17/9 is changed to 18/10. System B uses 20/10 and 55/20 and performs similarly under small parameter changes. Which should you trust, and why?

Answer

Trust B. A performance collapse from a one-day parameter change means the returns depend on a very narrow range — almost certainly overfitting to history, which will break in live conditions. B demonstrates robustness: similar results under nearby parameters, which is more likely to reflect real market structure rather than noise.

Scoring guide#

  • 14–18 correct: you have the core arithmetic; start paper trading.
  • 10–13 correct: revisit Lessons 9–13, focusing on N, sizing and pyramiding.
  • Below 10: do not rush into live markets. Copy the rules in Appendix A by hand and walk through ten historical trades manually.
