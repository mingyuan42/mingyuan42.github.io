实验的结局How the Experiment Ended

本课目标：看清海龟实验如何提前结束，理解“理论上算出的风险”和“真实发生的风险”之间可以差多远，以及大师为什么会败给自己教出来的学生。

一、等趋势的日子里，有人在做事#

海龟们的日常，很大一部分是“等”。等一个突破，等趋势走出来，等止损被触发。多数人在这段时间看体育版、打乒乓。

第二期的四个人没有。汤姆·香克斯（Tom Shanks）、保罗·拉巴尔、厄尔·基弗、伊里·“乔治”·斯沃博达（Jiri Svoboda）组成了一个小组，想验证自己每天执行的规则到底对不对——不是质疑，而是用数据检验。

他们花了差不多一年，用最早的苹果电脑搭出一个系统测试平台，把 S1 和 S2 两套系统合并起来回测。

二、结论：老师算错了一倍#

结果让整个项目震动。

按丹尼斯和埃克哈特的设想，两套系统合并后，最坏情况下的回撤大约是 50%。但回测显示，实际最坏回撤接近 80%。

原因是：当 S1 和 S2 同时给出入场信号时，两组仓位会叠加，风险不是相加那么温和，而是在最糟糕的时刻集中爆发。拉巴尔凭直觉就锁定了这一点。

研究小组把这个结论告诉德卢特里。据说德卢特里以前常对海龟半开玩笑地说：“你们要是真研究出什么，拿来给我们看。”他大概没想过真会发生。

丹尼斯和埃克哈特的第一反应，是那句所有行业老手都会说的话：“我们懂这个洞穴，我们比任何人都懂。”以他们的战绩，这么想太正常了。

但研究小组的结论很硬：他们错了一倍。 注意这里的“错了一倍”不是“错了 100 倍”，而是实际下的注是以为的两倍——丹尼斯自己在备忘录里写的是“你们一直在按我们以为的两倍规模交易”。

三、1986 年 4 月 23 日的备忘录#

德卢特里先下令：所有人把仓位规模削减 50%。随后是丹尼斯的正式备忘录，日期 1986 年 4 月 23 日：

真实世界的回撤远远超过理论预期值，这迫使我们必须重新评估“ theory 与事实”在“该做多大”这个问题上的联系。看起来我们误读了理论数据，以至于你们一直在按我们以为的两倍规模交易。好消息是，整个项目期间都是如此——你们的利润翻倍了，代价是风险也翻倍了。我们真是命大。

这段话值得逐句咀嚼：

  • “真实回撤远超理论预期”：模型没说谎，是模型被误读了。
  • “按两倍规模交易”：这不是小数点点错，而是风险预算整体翻倍。
  • “利润翻倍，风险也翻倍”：他们不是“侥幸赚了钱”，而是一直在承担双倍风险，只是赶上了好行情。
  • “我们真是命大”：一个管理上亿美元的人，用这四个字总结自己的风险控制。

执行上很简单：原来当 100 万美元用的账户，现在当 50 万美元用。规则一个字没改，只是杠杆降了一半。

四、减仓之后：学生开始赢老师#

更尴尬的事情发生了：砍掉一半仓位后，海龟们照样赚钱，而且作为一个群体，这段时间的表现超过了丹尼斯本人。

原因可能有很多：他们被训练出了好习惯；他们更严格地照规则做；丹尼斯还带着交易池时代的旧习气——香农说，丹尼斯不会发火，但会对成交滑点（skid）变得异常挑剔，那种在意在场上很重要，在场外就没那么重要了。

丹尼斯自己也隐约感到不对劲，他说过一句后来被证明像预言的话：

我骨子里其实是个逆势者，这对一个机械趋势跟随者来说，大概不是好事。

五、1988：传真机上的句号#

1988 年初，海龟项目被突然叫停。丹尼斯发了一份传真：项目结束。

背景是一连串坏消息：

  • 他替迈克尔·米尔肯的德雷塞尔·伯纳姆·兰伯特（Drexel Burnham Lambert）管理两只公开基金，亏损严重，随后关闭。
  • 1987 年 10 月股灾，他的基金 9 月 −15.29%、10 月 −13.79%。
  • 1988 年 4 月，他的公开基金单月 −55.49%。
  • 同期他父亲去世。有海龟把停课归因于“家庭原因”。

而同一个月，海龟们普遍只亏了 10%–12%。同一个市场，老师亏掉一半，学生亏掉一成。

迪马里亚回忆那天的错愕：“突然就结束了，就这么快。周一早上他们进来说，‘到周五，我们就结束了。’我心想，哦，得去找份工作了。”

关于为什么停，官方从未定义。有人说是德雷塞尔崩盘的连带后果，有人说是个人原因，丹尼斯自己的说法是：他退休了，要全职投入政治理念。

六、这一课真正的教训#

第一，风险预算会算错，而且错得很自然。 两套系统单独看都合理，合并起来却在同一时刻放大仓位。这是组合层面的风险，不是单笔交易的风险。任何多策略、多信号的系统，都必须问一句：它们会不会同时亮灯？

第二，回测不是装饰，是保险。 四个“学生”花一年搭测试平台，推翻了两位大师的假设。这不是叛逆，这是工程纪律。

第三，大师也会被自己的强项反噬。 丹尼斯的直觉让他在交易池里封神，但直觉也让他相信“我们懂这个洞穴”。当证据出现时，第一反应是防御，而不是更新。

第四，回撤是需要被管理的预期，不只是数字。 丹尼斯怪客户“不理解我的风格、一回撤就失去信心”。这句话有一半道理，但也暴露了问题：如果你不提前告诉别人可能亏多少，你就没有资格怪别人恐慌。

一个 −55.49% 的月份是什么概念？从剩下一半的净值回到原点，需要上涨约 124.6%（1 ÷ 0.4451 − 1）。这就是为什么风险管理里，“避免大亏”永远比“追求大赚”优先。

本课要点#

  • 四名海龟自建回测平台，发现 S1 与 S2 同时入场时风险叠加，实际最坏回撤约 80%，而非预期的 50%。
  • 丹尼斯 1986 年 4 月 23 日备忘录：仓位整体减半，因为一直在按两倍规模交易。
  • 减仓后海龟仍持续盈利，群体表现一度超过丹尼斯。
  • 1988 年初项目以一份传真结束；丹尼斯 1988 年 4 月单月亏损 55.49%，海龟同期约 −10%~−12%。
  • 教训：组合相关性、回测验证、警惕强项反噬、提前沟通回撤预期。

自测#

  1. 为什么“S1 和 S2 各自都合理”仍然可能导致 80% 的回撤？
  2. 丹尼斯说“利润翻倍，风险也翻倍，我们真是命大”。这句话里“命大”具体指什么？
  3. 一个月亏 55.49% 后，需要多大的涨幅才能回本？这个数字说明了什么？
参考答案

1. 因为两套系统可能在同一时刻对同一市场给出同方向信号，仓位叠加，风险在最不利的时点集中。单系统风险合格，不等于组合风险合格。

2. 指他们在承担双倍风险的情况下，恰好遇上了有利行情，所以没有先被回撤打死。赚钱的结果掩盖了风险控制上的错误，这是运气而非能力。

3. 剩余净值 44.51%，需上涨 1/0.4451 − 1 ≈ 124.6%。说明亏损与回本不对称：跌得越深，恢复所需收益越呈非线性放大，所以控制回撤优先于追求收益。

What you will learn: how the Turtle program ended early, how far "risk on paper" can drift from "risk in reality," and why a master lost to the students he created.

1. While everyone waited for a trend, four of them worked#

Much of a Turtle's day was waiting: for a breakout, for a trend, for a stop. Most passed the time with the sports pages and Ping-Pong.

Four members of the second class did not. Tom Shanks, Paul Rabar, Erle Keefer and Jiri "George" Svoboda formed a small research group. Their goal was not to doubt the rules but to test them with data.

It took about a year, using early Apple computers, to build a systems-testing platform. Then they blended S1 and S2 and ran the history.

2. The finding: the teachers were off by a factor of two#

The result shook the program.

As Dennis and Eckhardt understood it, running both systems together carried a worst-case drawdown of about 50%. The tests showed a worst-case drawdown closer to 80%.

The reason: when S1 and S2 both fired an entry in the same market at the same time, the two positions stacked. Risk did not merely add; it concentrated at the worst possible moment. Rabar spotted this intuitively.

The group told Dale Dellutri, who had once joked, "If you guys ever invent anything, bring it to us." He had not really expected it to happen.

Dennis and Eckhardt's first reaction was the reflex of every seasoned operator: "We know this trading cave, and we know it better than anyone else." With their record, that was a natural thought.

But the finding was blunt: they were off by a factor of 100 percent — that is, the Turtles had been trading twice the intended size. Dennis's own memo says it plainly: "you have been trading as much as twice as big as we thought."

3. The memo of April 23, 1986#

Dellutri first ordered everyone to cut position size by 50%. Then came the official memo from Dennis, dated April 23, 1986:

Real-world drawdowns far in excess of theoretically expected values have caused us to reassess the linkage between theory and fact regarding how big to trade. It seems that we have misconstrued the theoretical data so that you have been trading as much as twice as big as we thought. The good news is that this has been true throughout the whole trading program — your profits were doubled, but at the cost of a doubling of the risk. We must be living right.

Read it line by line:

  • "Drawdowns far in excess of theoretically expected values" — the model did not lie; it was misread.
  • "Twice as big as we thought" — not a decimal error, but a risk budget doubled across the board.
  • "Profits doubled, at the cost of a doubling of the risk" — they were not lucky winners; they had been running double risk in a friendly market.
  • "We must be living right" — a man managing hundreds of millions summarising his risk control with four words about luck.

Execution was simple: a $1 million account was now traded as if it were $500,000. Not one rule changed; the leverage was halved.

4. After the cut: students beating the teacher#

Then came the awkward part. With half the size, the Turtles kept making money — and as a group, they outperformed Dennis himself during this period.

There are several explanations: they had been drilled into good habits; they followed the rules more strictly; Dennis still carried pit-era reflexes. Shannon noted that Dennis would not get angry but became hyper-critical about "skid," the quality of fills — crucial in the pit, less so off the floor.

Dennis sensed something himself. He once said, prophetically:

I really am a contrarian at heart, and that's really probably not good if you're a mechanical trend follower.

5. 1988: a full stop by fax#

In early 1988 the program was suddenly shut down. Dennis sent a fax: it was over.

The background was a chain of bad news:

  • He ran two public funds with Michael Milken's Drexel Burnham Lambert; they closed with large losses.
  • The October 1987 crash hit him hard: −15.29% in September, −13.79% in October.
  • In April 1988 his public fund lost 55.49% in a single month.
  • His father had recently died; one Turtle attributed the shutdown to "family issues."

In that same month, the Turtles typically lost 10–12%. Same market: the teacher down by half, the students down by a tenth.

Jim DiMaria recalled the shock: "All of a sudden it's over. That's how fast it was. They came in Monday morning and said, 'Friday, we're done.' I was like, 'Oh, better get a job.'"

The reason for the shutdown was never officially defined. Some point to the Drexel collapse, some to personal circumstances. Dennis said simply that he was retiring to pursue politics full time.

6. The real lessons#

First, risk budgets get miscalculated, and the error is natural. Each system looked sound alone; together they stacked at the same moment. That is portfolio-level risk, not trade-level risk. Any multi-system, multi-signal approach must ask: will they fire at the same time?

Second, backtesting is not decoration; it is insurance. Four "students" spent a year building a test platform and overturned their teachers' assumption. That is not rebellion; it is engineering discipline.

Third, a master can be undone by his own strength. Dennis's intuition made him legendary in the pit, and the same intuition told him "we know this cave." When evidence arrived, the first response was defence, not updating.

Fourth, drawdown is an expectation to be managed, not just a number. Dennis blamed clients for "not understanding my style" and losing faith during drawdowns. There is half a truth there — but also a failure: if you never told people how much they could lose, you have no right to be surprised when they panic.

What does a −55.49% month mean? To get back to even from the remaining 44.51%, you need a gain of about 124.6% (1 ÷ 0.4451 − 1). That asymmetry is why risk management puts "avoid the big loss" ahead of "chase the big win."

Key points#

  • Four Turtles built a backtest platform and found that S1 and S2 stacking produced ~80% worst-case drawdowns, not 50%.
  • Dennis's April 23, 1986 memo halved position sizes because the program had been trading double the intended risk.
  • After the cut the Turtles kept performing and, as a group, briefly beat Dennis.
  • The program ended by fax in early 1988; Dennis lost 55.49% in April 1988 while Turtles lost roughly 10–12%.
  • Lessons: portfolio correlation, verification by backtest, the danger of one's own strengths, and communicating drawdown expectations in advance.

Self-test#

  1. Why can two individually sound systems still produce an 80% drawdown together?
  2. Dennis wrote that profits doubled but risk doubled too, and "we must be living right." What exactly does "living right" mean here?
  3. After a 55.49% monthly loss, what gain is needed to get back to even, and what does that imply?
Answers

1. Both systems can signal the same direction in the same market at the same moment, stacking positions and concentrating risk at the worst time. Sound single-system risk does not imply sound portfolio risk.

2. They survived on favourable markets while carrying twice the intended risk. The profits masked a control error; the outcome was luck, not skill.

3. 44.51% remains, so the required gain is 1/0.4451 − 1 ≈ 124.6%. Losses and recoveries are asymmetric: the deeper the drawdown, the nonlinearly larger the return needed. Hence control drawdown before chasing return.
