谁是「韭菜」？Who Is a “Leek”?

一种割了还会再长的菜，怎么就成了交易者的外号？How did a vegetable that grows back after cutting become a nickname for traders?

先讲个故事First, a story

韭菜是一种很特别的菜。你把叶子割掉，过几天它又长出来，你还能再割一次。一茬接一茬，好像永远割不完。A leek is a special vegetable. You cut off its leaves. A few days later new leaves grow back, and you can cut it again. Row after row, it never seems to run out.

市场上的人借用了这个画面：有些新手带着钱进来，亏了；过一阵子又带着钱进来，又亏了。他们像韭菜一样「割了又长」。于是大家叫他们「韭菜」，亏钱叫「被割」。People in the market borrowed that picture. Some new traders bring money in and lose it. Later they bring money in again, and lose it again. Like leeks, they grow back after being cut. So people call them leeks, and losing money is called getting cut.

作者李笑来说，他自己一开始也这样。2011 年他第一次买比特币，涨了一点就赶紧卖掉一大半，还觉得自己「跑得快」；剩下的放在一家交易所里，结果那家交易所被黑客攻击，币没了。这段丢人的经历，他写在了书的附录里。The author, Li Xiaolai, says he started out the same way. He first bought bitcoin in 2011. When the price rose a little he quickly sold most of it and felt proud of getting out in time. The rest sat on an exchange. That exchange was hacked, and those coins were gone. He put this embarrassing story in the appendix of the book.

这一课的那句话The one line to keep

韭菜不是「钱少的人」，而是「脑子里装着一个错想法的人」。A leek is not a person with little money. A leek is a person carrying one wrong idea.

三个关键词Three key words

韭菜Leek

市场里反复亏钱、又反复带着钱回来的新手。A new trader who keeps losing money and keeps coming back with more.

被割Getting cut

亏钱的那一刻，就像叶子碰上镰刀。The moment you lose money — like leaves meeting a sickle.

零和游戏Zero-sum game

你赢多少、别人就输多少的游戏，比如猜拳赌糖。书里说：市场长期看不是这种游戏。A game where your win is exactly someone else's loss, like betting candy on rock-paper-scissors. The book says the market is not that kind of game over the long run.

换成生活里的事The same thing in daily life

游戏里的「再来一局」“One more round” in a game

输了就再充一次值，却从不看回放、不改打法——这就是「割了又长」。You lose, you buy more coins, but you never watch the replay or change how you play. That is growing back to be cut again.

盲盒卡片Blind-box cards

一直买一直不中，心里只想「下一次一定中」。想法没变，结果就不会变。You keep buying and never pull the card you want, thinking “next time for sure.” If the idea never changes, the result never changes.

你可能会问Questions you might ask

钱很多的人就不会被割吗？Can a rich person still get cut?

会。书在最后把韭菜重新定义为：在一个本来不是零和的游戏里，偏偏以为自己在玩零和游戏的人。跟钱多钱少没关系，跟脑子里的假设有关。Yes. At the end the book defines a leek as a trader who wrongly believes a non-zero-sum market is a zero-sum game. It is not about how much money you have. It is about the assumption in your head.

被割过一次，就永远是韭菜了吗？If you get cut once, are you a leek forever?

不是。结语里作者说：「你不是韭菜，你从来都不是韭菜，最多是差点成了韭菜。」被割一次是学费，反复被割才是宿命。No. In the conclusion the author writes: you are not a leek, you have never been a leek, at most you almost became one. Being cut once is tuition. Being cut again and again is the fate.

作者为什么要写这本书？Why did the author write this book?

因为一段私下聊天被录音泄露，网上把他写成了「割韭菜的人」。他想说清楚：真正割你的不是某个人，而是你自己脑子里那个错想法。他还把书开源，请大家用原书名到处转发。A private conversation was recorded and leaked, and articles online painted him as the man cutting the leeks. He wanted to make one thing clear: the thing cutting you is not a person, it is the wrong idea in your own head. He also made the book open source and asked readers to share it everywhere under its original title.

今天的练习Today's practice

写下你生活里一件「割了又长」的事（游戏、零食、盲盒都算），再写一句「下次我改做什么」。Write down one thing in your life that grows back to be cut — a game, snacks, blind boxes. Then write one sentence: next time I will do this instead.

读完了就标记一下，课程首页的进度条会记住。Mark it when you finish — the progress bar on the course home remembers.
