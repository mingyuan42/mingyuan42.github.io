管好自己的事业Mind Your Own Business

麦当劳的创始人对着一屋子 MBA 学生说：我不做汉堡生意。那他做的是什么生意？The founder of McDonald's told a room of MBA students: I am not in the hamburger business. So what business was he in?

先讲个故事First, a story

1974 年，麦当劳创始人雷·克洛克去德州大学给 MBA 班讲课。课后大家去喝啤酒，他问：你们觉得我是做什么生意的？学生们笑：全世界都知道你做汉堡啊。他说：不，我做的是房地产生意。In 1974 Ray Kroc, the founder of McDonald's, spoke to an MBA class at the University of Texas. Over beers afterwards he asked: what business am I in? The students laughed — everyone knows you sell hamburgers. He said: no, my business is real estate.

他解释：卖加盟权是表面，真正要紧的是每家店的位置。加盟商买下的不只是生意，还有那块地。今天麦当劳拥有的房地产比天主教会还多，握着全世界最值钱的路口和街角。Selling franchises was the surface, he explained; the location of each store was the real prize. A franchise buyer was also buying the land underneath. Today McDonald's owns more real estate than the Catholic church, holding some of the most valuable intersections on earth.

这就是第三个秘密：管好自己的事业。作者说，很多人一辈子在替别人忙：先替公司老板赚，再替政府交税，最后替银行还房贷。忙到最后，自己什么也没剩下。That is the third secret: mind your own business. Most people, the author says, spend their lives working for everyone else — first the company's owners, then the government through taxes, then the bank that holds the mortgage. At the end there is nothing left of their own.

他区分了两个词：职业和事业。你在银行上班，那是职业；你拥有那家银行吗？没有的话，你还需要自己的事业。事业不在工资栏里，在资产栏里。「我需要加薪」「我要加班」「我再打一份工」——这些都还在工资栏里打转。He separates two words: profession and business. You work at a bank — that is your profession. Do you own the bank? If not, you still need your own business. Your business lives in the asset column, not the income column. “I need a raise”, “I'll work overtime”, “I'll take a second job” all still circle the income column.

他的建议不是辞职，而是：保住白天的工作，当一个好员工，同时一块钱一块钱地建资产栏。每一块钱进了资产栏，就成了你的员工——它一天工作二十四小时，还能替你的下一代工作。His advice is not to quit. Keep the day job, be a great employee, and build the asset column one dollar at a time. Every dollar that enters the asset column becomes your employee: it works twenty-four hours a day and can keep working for your children.

这一课的那句话The one line to keep

职业是你白天做的事，事业是你资产栏里长出来的东西。Your profession is what you do by day. Your business is what grows in your asset column.

三个关键词Three key words

职业 ≠ 事业Profession is not business

「我是银行家」通常意思是「我在银行上班」。职业是你替谁干活，事业是你拥有什么。“I'm a banker” usually means “I work at a bank”. Your profession is who you work for; your business is what you own.

资产栏The asset column

账本右边那一栏，专门放会下蛋的东西。书里的规矩：一块钱进去了，就别让它出来。The column on your balance sheet reserved for things that lay eggs. The book's rule: once a dollar goes in, never let it come back out.

奢侈品最后买Luxuries come last

穷人和中产先买大房子、名表、跑车，为了看起来有钱；真正的富人先建资产，再用资产产生的钱买这些。The poor and middle class buy the big house, the watch and the car first, to look rich. The long-term rich build assets first and let the assets pay for those things.

书里的清单Lists from the book

还在工资栏里打转的说法Sentences that still circle the income column

  1. 我需要加薪。I need a raise.
  2. 要是能升职就好了。If only I had a promotion.
  3. 我再去读个学位，好找份更好的工作。I'll go back to school for a better job.
  4. 我要加班。I'll work overtime.
  5. 我再打一份工。Maybe I can get a second job.

作者自己的做法What the author actually did

  1. 在海军陆战队和施乐上班的同时，一直在买卖房产和小公司股票。While serving in the Marine Corps and working at Xerox, he kept trading property and small-company shares.
  2. 把销售提成投进资产，而不是花掉。同事以为他在挥霍，其实他在买资产。He put his sales commissions into assets instead of spending them. Colleagues thought he was blowing the money.
  3. 不到三年，房产公司赚的超过了施乐的工资。In under three years his property company earned more than his Xerox salary.
  4. 第一辆保时捷是公司买的，不是他自己掏的。His first Porsche was bought by the company, not out of his own pocket.

换成生活里的事The same thing in daily life

球队队员 vs 组织比赛的人Player vs organiser

你是班级篮球队员，这是「职业」；你组织了一个每周六的联赛，排赛程、找场地、记比分，就算你请假比赛也照常进行，这是「事业」。区别不在辛苦程度，在你不在的时候它还转不转。Playing for the class team is a profession. Running a Saturday league — fixtures, pitch, scorekeeping — that still happens when you are absent, is a business. The difference is not effort; it is whether it runs without you.

先买游戏机，还是先做能赚钱的东西Console first, or build first

想要一台游戏机，两条路：借钱买，然后每月还；或者先做一件能持续带来零花钱的事，等它攒够了再买。第二条慢，但买完之后你手上多了一台会下蛋的机器，而不是一笔债。You want a games console. Road one: borrow, then repay monthly. Road two: build something that brings in pocket money, wait until it has paid for the console. Slower — but at the end you own a machine that lays eggs, not a debt.

「如果不喜欢，就不会好好照顾它」“If you don't love it, you won't take care of it”

富爸爸让作者买自己喜欢的资产。作者喜欢房子和土地，可以看一下午。你喜欢什么？恐龙、乐高、画画、代码？从你真心喜欢的东西开始，你才愿意长期照顾它。Rich dad told him to acquire assets he loved. Robert loves buildings and land and could look at them all day. What do you love — dinosaurs, bricks, drawing, code? Start there, because you will only look after something for years if you actually like it.

你可能会问Questions you might ask

小学生哪来的资产？What assets could a kid possibly have?

现在几乎没有，这很正常。这一课的重点不是让你去买什么，而是让你从小学会问：这件事我是在替别人的系统出力，还是在建自己的系统？一本你写的、同学愿意借阅的小册子，就是最小号的资产。Almost none yet, and that is fine. This lesson is not about buying anything. It is about learning to ask: am I feeding someone else's system, or building my own? A booklet you write that classmates want to borrow is the smallest possible asset.

那是不是应该鼓励大家都去开公司？Should everyone start a company then?

书里明确反对。作者说：十家公司里九家五年内会倒，活下来的再有九成会倒。除非你真的很想开，否则保住工作、管好自己的资产栏就好。The book explicitly says no. Nine out of ten companies fail within five years, and nine out of ten survivors fail later. Unless you truly want to run one, keep your job and mind your asset column.

「净资产一百万」为什么可能不准？Why can “net worth of a million” be misleading?

作者说，很多所谓的资产一旦真要卖，只能卖出账面价的一小部分，赚了还要交税。所以他的净资产常常「不值那么多」。真正靠得住的是每个月进账的现金流，不是表格上的数字。Because many listed assets sell for a fraction of their paper value, and any gain is taxed. So net worth is often worth less than it looks. What you can rely on is monthly cash flow, not a number on a form.

今天的练习Today's practice

写下你现在的「职业」（学生？值日生？球队队员？），再写下一个可能的「小事业」——一件你搭好之后能自己运转的事。这周只搭最小的一版。Write down your current profession (student? monitor? team player?), then one possible small business — something that keeps running once you have set it up. Build only the smallest version this week.

读完了就标记一下，课程首页的进度条会记住。Mark it when you finish — the progress bar on the course home remembers.

这是一门读书课，不是投资建议。书里的具体买卖例子发生在几十年前的美国，规则、市场和风险都和今天不同；作者自己也反复说「我做的不推荐给你」。我们只学他的思考方式，练习全部放在生活里，不涉及任何真实买卖。This is a reading course, not investment advice. The book's deals happened in America decades ago, under different rules, markets and risks — the author himself keeps saying he does not recommend them. We only borrow his way of thinking. Every exercise here happens in daily life, never in real trading.
