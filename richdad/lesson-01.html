两个爸爸，两种想法Two Dads, Two Ways of Thinking

一样努力、一样聪明的两个人，为什么一个留下几千万，一个留下一堆账单？Two men, equally hardworking and equally smart. Why did one leave millions behind, and the other a stack of unpaid bills?

先讲个故事First, a story

罗伯特九岁的时候，身边有两位爸爸。一位是他的亲爸爸：读到博士，在斯坦福、芝加哥大学、西北大学拿全额奖学金念书，后来当上夏威夷的教育官员。另一位是他好朋友迈克的爸爸：八年级都没读完。When Robert was nine he had two dads. One was his own father: a Ph.D. who studied at Stanford, the University of Chicago and Northwestern on full scholarships, and later ran education for the state of Hawaii. The other was his best friend Mike's father, who never finished eighth grade.

两个人都很努力，都赚了不少钱，都很有影响力。可是亲爸爸一辈子在钱上发愁，去世后留下的是要还的账单；迈克的爸爸成了夏威夷最有钱的人之一，去世后留下几千万给家人、慈善机构和教堂。Both worked hard all their lives. Both earned good incomes. Both were strong, influential men. Yet his own father struggled with money and left bills to be paid. Mike's father became one of the richest men in Hawaii and left tens of millions to his family, to charities and to his church.

差别不在工资单上，在他们嘴上。亲爸爸常说「我买不起」，富爸爸禁止家里说这句话，一定要改成问句：「我怎样才买得起？」一个是句号，说完大脑就下班了；一个是问号，说完大脑才开始上班。The difference was not in their paychecks. It was in their sentences. His own father said, “I can't afford it.” Rich dad banned that sentence at home and made the boys ask instead: “How can I afford it?” One is a full stop — the brain clocks out. The other is a question mark — the brain clocks in.

亲爸爸说「我永远也不会有钱」，这句话后来真的应验了。富爸爸哪怕刚刚赔光、口袋空空，也还是说「我是个有钱人」。他有一句口头禅：没钱是暂时的，穷是一辈子的。His own father said, “I'll never be rich,” and that prophecy came true. Rich dad kept calling himself a rich man even right after a setback left him flat broke. His line was: being broke is temporary, being poor is eternal.

九岁的罗伯特要选一个听。他选了那位没有学历的富爸爸，跟着学了整整三十年，直到三十九岁。他说这个决定很痛苦，但改变了他的一生——就像诗人弗罗斯特写的那句：林子里分出两条路，我选了人少的那一条，从此一切都不一样了。At nine, Robert had to choose whom to listen to. He chose the dad without the degrees, and learned from him for thirty years, until he was thirty-nine. He says the choice was painful and changed his life — like the Robert Frost lines he quotes: two roads diverged in a wood, he took the one less traveled by, and that has made all the difference.

这一课的那句话The one line to keep

决定你钱包的，不是工资单，是你脑子里那几句话。It is not your paycheck that decides your wallet. It is the sentences in your head.

三个关键词Three key words

财务教育Financial education

关于钱怎么运作的知识。学校教语文数学和专业技能，却几乎不教这个，所以很多成绩很好的医生、银行家也会一辈子缺钱。Knowledge about how money works. School teaches reading, maths and job skills but almost none of this, which is why clever doctors and bankers can still struggle with money all their lives.

「我怎样才买得起？」“How can I afford it?”

把一句放弃的话改成一道题。不是让你想买什么就买什么，而是逼大脑去想办法——大脑越练越强。Turn a sentence that gives up into a problem to solve. It does not mean buy everything you want. It means make your brain work — and a brain that works gets stronger.

穷 ≠ 没钱Poor is not broke

没钱是这一刻口袋里空了，明天可能就有；穷是一种一直不变的想法。书里说：没钱是暂时的，穷是一辈子的。Broke means your pocket is empty today and might be full tomorrow. Poor is a way of thinking that never changes. The book says: broke is temporary, poor is eternal.

换成生活里的事The same thing in daily life

「我数学不行」“I'm bad at maths”

这句话和「我买不起」是同一个句式：说完就不用再想了。换成「我怎样才能把这类题做对？」，你的大脑会立刻开始找办法：问老师、找同类题、每天练五道。句子换了，行动就出来了。This is the same shape as “I can't afford it”: once you say it, you stop thinking. Swap it for “How can I get this kind of problem right?” and your brain starts hunting: ask the teacher, find similar problems, do five a day. Change the sentence and the actions appear.

游戏里的两条路Two roads in a game

很多游戏都有两种玩法：一直刷小怪攒金币，或者先花时间造一台自动产金币的机器。前者今天就能爽，后者前几天很枯燥。书里说的两条路，就是这个意思。Many games offer two ways to play: keep grinding small monsters for coins, or spend a few boring days building a machine that makes coins on its own. The first feels good today. The second is dull at first. That is exactly the fork this book describes.

饭桌上能不能谈钱Talking about money at dinner

穷爸爸禁止吃饭时谈钱，富爸爸鼓励在饭桌上聊生意。你也可以试试：今天晚饭问爸爸妈妈一个关于钱的问题，比如「我们家每个月最大的一笔开销是什么？」Poor dad banned money talk at the table. Rich dad encouraged it. Try it tonight: ask your parents one money question, like “What is the biggest thing our family pays for each month?”

你可能会问Questions you might ask

那穷爸爸是坏人吗？So was poor dad a bad person?

完全不是。书里说两位爸爸都正直、努力、慷慨，都爱他。穷爸爸的问题不是人品，是他从小接受的那套关于钱的想法。作者写这本书，正是因为他爱这位爸爸，也心疼他一辈子为钱发愁。Not at all. The book says both dads were honest, hardworking and generous, and both loved him. Poor dad's problem was not his character — it was the money ideas he grew up with. Robert wrote the book partly because he loved that dad and hated watching him worry.

所以读书没用？So is school useless?

不是。两位爸爸都非常重视教育，只是对「该学什么」意见不同。作者反对的是「学校只教专业技能、完全不教钱」，不是反对读书。他自己也一直在上课、买书、听讲座。No. Both dads valued education deeply; they disagreed about what to learn. The author objects to schools teaching job skills and nothing about money — not to learning itself. He kept taking classes, buying books and attending seminars his whole life.

有钱就一定是好人吗？Does being rich make you good?

书里没这么说，我们也不这么认为。钱是一种能力，不是品德。这门课学的是「让钱为你工作」的思路，至于用这些能力去做什么，是你自己的选择。The book never says that, and neither do we. Money is a capability, not a character. This course teaches the idea of making money work for you. What you do with that capability is your own choice.

今天的练习Today's practice

今天抓一句自己常说的「我做不到 / 我买不起 / 我不行」，把它改写成「我怎样才能……？」，然后在纸上写出三个可能的办法。哪怕办法很傻也写下来——重点是让大脑开始上班。Catch one sentence you say often — “I can't”, “I can't afford it”, “I'm no good at this” — and rewrite it as “How can I…?” Then write down three possible answers, even silly ones. The point is to put your brain back on the clock.

对照Compare

同一件事，两种说法Same topic, two answers
穷爸爸（高学历）Poor dad (the Ph.D.)
富爸爸（八年级）Rich dad (grade eight)
贪财是万恶之源。The love of money is the root of all evil.
缺钱才是万恶之源。The lack of money is the root of all evil.
我买不起。I can't afford it.
我怎样才买得起？How can I afford it?
好好读书，找一家好公司去上班。Study hard so you can find a good company to work for.
好好读书，找一家好公司把它买下来。Study hard so you can find a good company to buy.
我不富有，是因为我有你们这些孩子。The reason I'm not rich is because I have you kids.
我必须富有，正是因为我有你们这些孩子。The reason I must be rich is because I have you kids.
钱的事，别冒险，稳一点。When it comes to money, play it safe. Don't take risks.
学会管理风险。Learn to manage risk.
房子是我们最大的资产。Our home is our largest investment and our greatest asset.
自住的房子是负债。My house is a liability.
先把账单付了，剩下的才是自己的。Pay the bills first, keep what is left.
先付给自己，再去付账单。Pay yourself first, then pay the bills.
我不为钱工作，钱不重要。I'm not interested in money. Money doesn't matter.
我不为钱工作，钱为我工作。I don't work for money. Money works for me.

本课对应原书

  • 引言富爸爸穷爸爸

这是一门读书课，不是投资建议。书里的具体买卖例子发生在几十年前的美国，规则、市场和风险都和今天不同；作者自己也反复说「我做的不推荐给你」。我们只学他的思考方式，练习全部放在生活里，不涉及任何真实买卖。This is a reading course, not investment advice. The book's deals happened in America decades ago, under different rules, markets and risks — the author himself keeps saying he does not recommend them. We only borrow his way of thinking. Every exercise here happens in daily life, never in real trading.
