五个拦路虎Five Obstacles

学会了所有道理，为什么还是动不了？书里说有五只老虎挡在路上。You have learned every principle — so why can you still not move? The book says five tigers block the road.

先讲个故事First, a story

作者说，就算一个人财务知识满分，还是有五样东西会让他原地不动。他把它们叫做拦路虎。第一只是恐惧：怕亏钱。他说，怕亏钱是对的，人人都怕；差别在于怕了之后怎么办。Even someone with perfect financial literacy can be stopped by five things, the author says. He calls them obstacles. The first is fear of losing money. Fear is normal — everyone has it. The difference is what you do after you feel it.

他打了个比方：德州人不怕输，他们怕的是「输得不够大」。意思是，真正让人富有的不是从不失败，而是失败之后还能站起来再试。他引用一句老话：失败的人，是那些从没赢过的人；而赢家，是那些输过但没停下的人。He uses a Texas saying: people there are not afraid of losing, they are afraid of losing too small. The point is that wealth comes not from never failing but from getting up after failure. His line: losers are those who never won; winners are those who lost but did not stop.

第二只是愤世嫉俗：什么都不信，什么都嘲笑。作者说，这种人看起来最聪明，其实最穷——因为他们把每一次机会都先否定掉。他叫他们「小鸡」：天要塌了！天要塌了！然后什么也不做。The second is cynicism: believing nothing, mocking everything. These people look the smartest and are often the poorest, because they reject every opportunity before examining it. He calls them chickens: the sky is falling! The sky is falling! — and then they do nothing.

第三只是懒惰。但作者说的懒惰不是躺着不动，而是「用忙碌掩盖懒惰」：一直加班、一直刷手机、一直说「我太忙了」，其实是在逃避真正该做的那件事。他给的解药很特别：不是「我要努力」，而是「我想要什么？」——一点点贪心，反而能治好懒惰。The third is laziness — but not lying still. It is busyness used as a cover: endless overtime, endless scrolling, endless “I'm too busy”, all avoiding the one thing that matters. His cure is unusual: not “I must work harder” but “What do I want?” A little honest greed can cure laziness.

第四只是坏习惯。我们的生活就是习惯的总和。作者说，先付给自己，再付账单——这个顺序会逼你想办法，而「先付账单，剩下的再说」会让你永远剩不下。第五只是傲慢：傲慢是无知加上自负。他说，每次他傲慢的时候，就是他亏钱的时候，因为他以为自己知道，其实不知道。The fourth is bad habits: our life is the sum of them. Pay yourself first, then the bills — that order forces you to find a way, while “bills first, keep the rest” leaves nothing. The fifth is arrogance, which he defines as ignorance plus ego. Every time he was arrogant, he lost money, because he believed he knew when he did not.

这一课的那句话The one line to keep

挡住你的不是不懂，是懂了之后那五种反应。What blocks you is not ignorance. It is the five reactions that come after knowing.

三个关键词Three key words

恐惧Fear

怕亏钱。解法不是「别怕」，而是「早点开始、小额试错」。作者说，对亏钱的恐惧人人都有，差别在于处理方式。Fear of losing. The answer is not “stop fearing” but “start early, fail small”. Everyone fears loss; the difference is how you handle it.

愤世嫉俗Cynicism

先否定，再分析，最后什么也不做。看起来聪明，实际上把机会全挡在门外。解法：先问「如果这是真的呢？」Reject first, analyse later, act never. Looks clever, blocks every opportunity. The fix: ask “what if this were true?” first.

懒惰Laziness

用忙碌掩盖逃避。解药是一句「我想要什么？」——承认自己的欲望，反而有力气行动。Busyness covering avoidance. The cure is one question: “What do I want?” Admitting the desire gives you the energy to move.

坏习惯Bad habits

先付账单还是先付自己，顺序不同，结果不同。习惯不是靠意志力改的，是靠换顺序改的。Bills first or yourself first — different order, different result. Habits change by reordering, not by willpower.

傲慢Arrogance

无知 + 自负 = 傲慢。作者说，他每次亏钱都是因为以为自己知道。解法：不懂的地方，请懂的人。Ignorance plus ego equals arrogance. He says every loss came from believing he knew. The fix: where you do not know, hire someone who does.

换成生活里的事The same thing in daily life

「天要塌了」型同学The “sky is falling” classmate

每次有人提一个新想法，他第一句总是「这不行」「早就有人试过了」。听起来很懂，其实什么也没做。下次听到这种话，试着问：「那如果可行呢？需要什么条件？」Every new idea gets “that won't work” or “someone already tried it”. Sounds knowledgeable, does nothing. Next time, try asking: “And if it could work — what would it need?”

「我太忙了」“I'm too busy”

留意这句话出现的时刻。很多时候它不是真的忙，而是不想面对那件更难的事。作者说，这就是懒惰最常见的伪装。Notice when this sentence appears. Often it is not real busyness but avoidance of the harder thing. The author says this is laziness's favourite disguise.

先给自己留一块Keep one piece for yourself first

零花钱一到手，先拿出十分之一放进「资产罐」，再安排其他。顺序一换，你会发现剩下的钱居然也够用——而且罐子在长大。When pocket money arrives, put one tenth into the asset jar first, then plan the rest. Change the order and you will find the remainder still covers things — while the jar grows.

你可能会问Questions you might ask

「先付给自己」会不会导致不付账单？Does “pay yourself first” mean skipping bills?

作者特别澄清过：不是不付账单，而是先留出投资的那一份，让剩下的压力逼你想办法。他还说，如果连账单都付不出，那说明该换思路了，而不是继续硬撑。真实世界里，欠债不还是违法的，这条只适用于「有余钱可分配」的情况。He clarifies: it does not mean skipping bills. It means setting aside the investing share first and letting the remaining pressure force solutions. He also says if you cannot pay the bills at all, you need a new plan, not more strain. In real life, not paying debts is illegal — this applies only when there is a surplus to allocate.

恐惧真的能治好吗？Can fear really be cured?

作者说不能，也不该。他说「对亏钱的恐惧人人都有」，差别在于处理它的方式。他的办法是早点开始、小额试错，让失败变成学费而不是灾难。He says no, and it should not be. Everyone fears losing money; the difference is how you handle it. His method is to start early and fail small, so failure becomes tuition instead of disaster.

傲慢和自信怎么分？How is arrogance different from confidence?

自信是「我知道我能学会」；傲慢是「我已经知道了，不用学」。前者会去问、去查、去请人；后者会拒绝一切新信息。作者说，他每次亏钱都发生在后者出现的时候。Confidence says “I know I can learn this.” Arrogance says “I already know, no need to learn.” The first asks, checks and hires; the second refuses new information. He says every loss happened when the second showed up.

今天的练习Today's practice

五只老虎里，哪一只最常出现在你身上？这周抓住它一次，写下当时的场景，然后用书里给的解药试一次。不用五只一起打，一次打一只。Which of the five tigers shows up most in you? Catch it once this week, write down the scene, and try the book's antidote. You do not need to fight all five at once — one at a time.

本课对应原书

  • 第 7 章克服障碍

这是一门读书课，不是投资建议。书里的具体买卖例子发生在几十年前的美国，规则、市场和风险都和今天不同；作者自己也反复说「我做的不推荐给你」。我们只学他的思考方式，练习全部放在生活里，不涉及任何真实买卖。This is a reading course, not investment advice. The book's deals happened in America decades ago, under different rules, markets and risks — the author himself keeps saying he does not recommend them. We only borrow his way of thinking. Every exercise here happens in daily life, never in real trading.
