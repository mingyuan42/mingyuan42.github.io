富人不为钱工作The Rich Don't Work for Money

一小时十美分，后来干脆一分钱不给——富爸爸为什么要这样「欺负」两个九岁小孩？Ten cents an hour, and later nothing at all. Why did rich dad treat two nine-year-olds this way?

10分钟min1个原书章节source chapters3张记忆卡cards2道测验题quiz
故事Story

罗伯特和迈克想学赚钱。他们先试着把牙膏皮熔了铸硬币，被亲爸爸拦下：那是造假币。于是他们去求迈克的爸爸。富爸爸说：可以，周六来我的超市干活，一小时十美分，每次三小时。Robert and Mike wanted to learn how to make money. First they melted toothpaste tubes to cast coins, until Robert's dad stopped them — that is counterfeiting. So they asked Mike's dad. Rich dad said yes: work Saturdays at my store, three hours, ten cents an hour.

三个星期后，罗伯特受不了了。三小时挣三毛钱，一本漫画就要一毛。他跑去富爸爸办公室，气呼呼地说要辞职，还说要举报他违反童工法。富爸爸笑了：这正是我要教你的第一课。Three weeks later Robert had had enough. Three hours of work bought thirty cents, and one comic cost ten. He marched into rich dad's office, furious, ready to quit and to report him for breaking child-labour laws. Rich dad smiled: this is exactly the first lesson.

富爸爸说，大多数人一辈子都在这个循环里：起床、上班、付账单；起床、上班、付账单。推着他们跑的是两种情绪——恐惧（怕没钱、怕被开除）和贪婪（想要更多好东西）。老板只要加一点工资，他们就继续跑，同时把开销也加大。他给这个循环起了个名字：老鼠赛跑。Most people, rich dad said, live inside one loop: get up, go to work, pay bills; get up, go to work, pay bills. Two emotions push them around it — fear of having no money, and greed for nicer things. Offer them a raise and they keep running, and raise their spending to match. He named the loop the Rat Race.

然后富爸爸把工资降到零：同样的三小时，一分钱不给。两个小孩又气又急，只好动脑筋。他们发现超市把卖不掉的漫画书封面撕下来退回给发行商，剩下的整本直接扔掉。他们要来这些「垃圾」，在迈克家地下室开了一个漫画图书馆，请迈克爱读书的妹妹当管理员，每个孩子交一毛钱，可以看两小时。Then rich dad cut the pay to zero: same three hours, no money at all. Angry and stuck, the boys had to use their heads. They noticed the store tore the covers off unsold comics for credit and threw the rest away. They asked for the “rubbish”, opened a comic-book library in Mike's basement, hired Mike's book-loving little sister as librarian, and charged each child ten cents for two hours of reading.

三个月里，图书馆平均每周赚九块五，付给妹妹一美元。最关键的是：罗伯特和迈克在店里干活的时候，图书馆照样在赚钱。他们第一次尝到「钱为我工作」的味道。后来因为打架，图书馆关了，但这一课已经学会了。For three months the library averaged $9.50 a week, and they paid the sister one dollar. The important part: while the boys were stacking shelves at the store, the library kept earning. That was their first taste of money working for them. A fight later closed the library, but the lesson had landed.

一句话The idea
为钱工作，方向盘在老板手里；让钱为你工作，方向盘才在你手里。Work for money and the steering wheel belongs to your boss. Make money work for you and the wheel is yours.
关键词Key words
老鼠赛跑The Rat Race
起床—上班—付账单的循环。像仓鼠在轮子里跑：腿飞快地动，笼子一点没变。加薪常常只是让轮子转得更快。The get-up, work, pay-bills loop. Like a hamster in a wheel: legs spinning fast, cage going nowhere. A raise often just spins the wheel faster.
恐惧与贪婪Fear and greed
两只看不见的手。恐惧让你不敢离开，贪婪让你把刚多的钱立刻花掉。认出它们，你才有机会不被它们牵着走。Two invisible hands. Fear keeps you from leaving; greed spends the extra money the moment it arrives. Naming them is the first step to not being led by them.
被动收入Passive income
你不在场也会进账的钱。漫画图书馆就是这样：罗伯特在超市擦货架，妹妹在地下室收门票。Money that arrives even when you are not there. The comic library worked like that: Robert was stacking shelves while his friend's sister collected the door money.
清单Lists
富爸爸给的三个选项The three options rich dad offered
  1. 接受十美分，继续干——大多数人一辈子选这个。Accept the ten cents and keep working — what most people choose for life.
  2. 嫌钱少，辞职，再找一份工作——换个笼子继续跑。Complain, quit, find another job — a new cage, same running.
  3. 不要工资，用脑子找出一个别人没看见的机会——两个小孩选了这条。Take no pay and use your head to find an opening nobody else sees — the road the two boys took.
三种收入（书末总结）Three kinds of income (from the closing pages)
  1. 劳动收入：你去上班换来的钱，税最重。Earned income: money you trade your hours for. Taxed the hardest.
  2. 投资组合收入：股票、债券这类纸面资产带来的钱。Portfolio income: money from paper assets such as stocks and bonds.
  3. 被动收入：房子租金这类你不在场也会来的钱，税最轻。Passive income: money like rent that arrives without you, taxed the least.
生活里In real life

值日生 vs 图书角Class monitor vs class library

每天帮老师搬作业本，是「用时间换认可」；在班里办一个图书角，制定借阅规则，让大家自己借自己还，是「造一个不用你天天守着的系统」。两件事都值得做，但第二种会一直运转下去。Carrying exercise books for the teacher every day trades your time for approval. Setting up a class library with borrowing rules that run without you builds a system. Both are worth doing — but only the second keeps running on its own.

刷怪 vs 造机器Grinding vs building

游戏里你可以一直刷小怪，也可以先花几天造一台自动农场。造机器的那几天很无聊，之后你睡觉它都在产出。这就是被动收入的感觉。In a game you can grind monsters forever, or spend a few dull days building an automatic farm. Once it is built it produces while you sleep. That is what passive income feels like.

零花钱一到手就没了Pocket money that vanishes

留意一下：钱一到手，是不是马上变成了零食和卡片？那不是贪婪的错，是习惯。试试把钱分成三份：一份现在花，一份存起来，一份用来做一件能「生出更多」的事。Notice what happens the moment pocket money lands: snacks and cards, instantly. That is habit, not a moral failing. Try splitting it three ways — spend some now, save some, and put some into something that could grow.

问一问Questions
所以不该去上班？So should nobody take a job?
书里从没这么说。作者自己在施乐卖了四年复印机，还多次强调「保住白天的工作，同时建自己的资产栏」。区别在于：你是被恐惧逼着上班，还是把工资当成搭建资产的材料。The book never says that. The author sold copiers at Xerox for four years and keeps repeating: keep your day job while you build your asset column. The difference is whether fear drives you to work, or whether you treat the paycheck as building material.
免费干活不就是被欺负吗？Isn't working for free just being exploited?
在真实世界里，是的，白干活是不对的，劳动应该得到报酬。书里这段是一个爸爸故意设计的教学情境，而且只持续了几周。我们学的是「别只盯着时薪，也看看能不能学到东西、能不能发现机会」，不是鼓励谁去白干活。In the real world, yes — work deserves pay. In the book this is a teaching situation a father deliberately designed, and it lasted only weeks. The lesson is “look beyond the hourly rate at what you can learn and spot”, not “go and work for free”.
漫画图书馆为什么算「钱为我工作」？Why did the comic library count as money working for them?
因为收钱的是妹妹，看店的是规则，两个男孩在别处。收入不再和「他们在不在场」绑定。这就是资产的定义：你不在，它照样往你口袋里放钱。Because the sister collected the money and the rules ran the room, while the boys were elsewhere. The income no longer depended on them being present. That is the definition of an asset: it puts money in your pocket even when you are not there.

动手做Your practice

想一件「你做一次、能一直帮到别人」的事：一份班级借阅表、一张错题清单、一个自动提醒的小装置。这周把它做出来，然后观察：你不在的时候，它还在起作用吗？Think of one thing you could build once that keeps helping people: a class borrowing sheet, a shared list of tricky problems, a small automatic reminder. Build it this week, then watch: does it still work when you are not there?
